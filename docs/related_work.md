# Related work and novelty check

Author: KEYUR SHAILESHKUMAR PATEL (ORCID https://orcid.org/0009-0008-5261-043X).
Conducted independently; not an official Brunel University London project.

Search done 2026-10-26 (web search, extended mode). Repeat it just before registering on OSF.

| Work | What it does | How this study differs |
|---|---|---|
| Ke et al. 2015, PNAS, [doi:10.1073/pnas.1424329112](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1424329112) | Defines beauty coefficient B; finds SBs in 22M papers from citation curves | Used here to build the sample; it does not try to wake SBs |
| Li, Ma & Feng 2024, J. Informetrics, [link](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/infome/v18y2024i2s1751157724000300.html) | Machine learning on citation trends to flag likely SBs and breakthroughs early (chemistry) | Predicts *which* papers will rise from citation features; does not read text or match a paper to a specific need |
| Chaleplioglou et al. 2026, Scientometrics, [doi:10.1007/s11192-026-05598-x](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11192-026-05598-x) | Retrospective: finds SBs awakened by COVID-19 mRNA vaccine work | After the fact; no prediction, no AI matching |
| Literature-based discovery (Swanson onward), e.g. [Recent advances in LBD, 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.12385); [evaluation critique](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9945845/) | Links concepts across literatures; time-sliced evaluation | Closest prior art. This study evaluates whole forgotten papers against an explicit need, with known SBs as ground truth, preregistered |
| [Elicit blog: hidden gems](https://elicit.com/blog/literature-based-discovery) | Argues AI could find overlooked papers | Proposal, not a test |
| Works in Progress, "Waking up science's sleeping beauties" | Argues AI literature mining could find SBs sooner | Proposal, not a test |

**Conclusion of the check:** no published study found that back-tests an AI engine on known sleeping
beauties using only pre-awakening information. LBD with time-slicing is the nearest prior work and must be
cited and discussed. "First" should be phrased as "to our knowledge, the first preregistered test of ...".
